    Face detection and recognition¶

    Two optional plugins. Together they let Minder say "Alex is at the main door" instead of "person at the main door" — and, more usefully, "someone unrecognised is at the gate".

    Neither is installed by default. Neither runs until you arm it.

    Plugin What it does Keeps
    face-detect says a face was visible, and how big nothing
    face-recognise puts a name to faces of people you enrolled one template per enrolled person

    If you only want "was there a face?", install the first and stop there. It stores nothing about anybody.

    Turning it on¶

    Three steps, and nothing happens until all three are done.

    1. Install the plugins. Models are fetched and checksum-verified at this point, not baked into the image.

    docker compose exec minder python -c "
import plugin_install as pi
print(pi.install('face-detect'))
print(pi.install('face-recognise'))"

    Installing does not start anything. It costs disk and nothing else.

    2. Arm them. This is what loads the models and starts the work.

    docker compose exec minder python -c "
import plugins
r = plugins.registry()
for p in ('face-detect', 'face-recognise'):
    ok, why = r.arm(p)
    print(p, '- armed' if ok else '- refused: ' + why)"

    If your CPU budget is already spent, this refuses and says by how much rather than quietly overloading the box.

    3. Switch plugins on for the appliance. In .env:

    MINDER_PLUGINS=on

    then ./minder up. This is the master switch — off by default, because optional perception on a machine whose first job is recording your house should be turned on deliberately.

    Check it worked¶

    docker compose exec minder python -c "
import plugins, plugin_bridge
r = plugins.registry()
print('plugins enabled:', plugin_bridge.ENABLED)
print('armed          :', [m.id for m in r.armed()])
print('cpu budget used:', f'{r.used_core_fraction():.1%}')"

    Then enrol someone at Portal → People, and the next time a camera sees a person Minder will try to name them.

    The rule that matters most¶

    A recognised face never silences an alert. It can change the words — "Alex at the main door" instead of "person at the main door" — but it cannot make an alert quieter, later, or absent.

    So if recognition gets it wrong, you get a wrong name, never a missed intruder. Your security does not depend on a face model being right.

    That means "don't alert me when it's me" is deliberately not built in. If you want it, you write it as a rule you can see and switch off — not as a hidden side effect of turning recognition on.

    What is stored, and what is not¶

    For each person you enrol:

    Stored Not stored
    a mathematical template (512 numbers) the photograph — discarded once the template is made
    their name and group anything about people you did not enrol
    who enrolled them, and when any record linking one stranger to another sighting

    The photograph goes as soon as the template is computed, including when enrolment fails. A template is not a picture and cannot be turned back into one.

    Nobody else gets a template — ever. An unrecognised face is recorded as "a face, matched nobody", and nothing about that person is kept.

    The cost of that, stated plainly¶

    You can ask: "was an unrecognised person at the gate last night?" You cannot ask: "was that the same stranger as Tuesday?"

    Answering the second would mean keeping face templates of people who never agreed to it. That is the trade, and it was chosen deliberately.

    Groups¶

    Everyone you enrol belongs to one group. Rules use the group, not the name — so "anyone who isn't family" keeps working the day a cousin visits.

    Group Typically
    family lives here; any hour is unremarkable
    household house help, nanny — expected, during known hours
    service gardener, cleaner, delivery
    guest temporary — expires, see below
    (no match) stranger — not a group, the absence of one

    You can add your own groups. A stranger needs no enrolment and no consent — it is simply a face that matched nobody, which is why stranger rules work for people who have never been near your house.

    Guests expire. A guest needs an end date, and the template is deleted automatically after it. Someone enrolled for a fortnight's visit will not still be recognised two years later.

    Enrolling someone¶

    Portal → People tab. One person at a time, from photographs — three to five, different angles and lighting, each with their face at least 80 pixels across. Nobody has to stand in front of a camera: enrol at the desk the way a reception would, and every camera recognises them.

    Enrolment only works from a signed-in browser session. Minder's own internal components cannot do it, and neither can the chat bot — storing someone's face is a decision a person makes, and the record says who made it.

    A photo with two faces in it is refused rather than guessed at.

    Deleting someone¶

    Portal → People → Delete. Minder then:

    1. removes every template for them;
    2. clears their name from past events — the events stay, only the identification is withdrawn;
    3. records the deletion, with who did it and how many things it touched;
    4. re-checks both stores and shows you the result.

    Step 4 is the point. The screen reports what a re-query found, not that a request succeeded.

    Writing rules¶

    Add to /data/rules.json, or use the Rules tab. Hours are optional — state them only if the rule cares.

    Alert if a stranger appears at night

    {"condition_type": "face_identity", "condition": "unrecognised person at night",
 "cameras": ["all"], "schedule": "22:00-06:00", "stranger": true, "enabled": true}

    Alert if anyone who is not family is here after 10pm

    {"condition_type": "face_identity", "condition": "not family, late",
 "cameras": ["all"], "schedule": "22:00-06:00", "group_not": "family", "enabled": true}

    Strangers count as "not family" — which is what you want, and would otherwise make the rule useless in the case you wrote it for.

    Tell me when the gardener arrives

    {"condition_type": "face_identity", "condition": "gardener here",
 "cameras": ["Front-Door"], "identity": "Ramesh", "enabled": true}
    Key Matches
    stranger: true a face that was looked at properly and matched nobody
    group_not: "family" anyone not in that group, including strangers
    group: "service" enrolled people in that group
    identity: "Ramesh" that person
    schedule always, night, day, or 22:00-06:00. Optional

    Combining with what Minder can already see¶

    "Alert if a stranger is carrying something" is two things, not one: the face plugin says a face matched nobody, and that triggers the second vision layer with a specific question about what the person is holding.

    Worth doing in that order — asking a vision model "is anyone dangerous?" of every frame is vague and expensive; asking "what is this person carrying?" of the rare 2am frame with an unrecognised face is cheap and sharp. The face plugin's job is turning a general question into a rare one.

    Three answers, not two¶

    Minder says Meaning
    a name that is who it is
    unrecognised a face, looked at properly, matching nobody
    too small to identify a face nobody could have identified

    The third is not a stranger, and does not fire stranger rules. Otherwise every distant figure at the end of a driveway would raise "unrecognised person", and you would turn the rule off within a week.

    What it costs¶

    Measured on the reference appliance (Intel N100, 8 cameras):
    grabbing a high-resolution frame 1.2–1.5s
    finding faces in it ~9ms*
    recognising one ~170ms
    while armed ~5% of one CPU core

    * Because Minder crops to the person Frigate already found: 2% of the pixels, at full resolution — 50x faster than scanning the whole frame, and sharper than shrinking it.

    The frame grab dominates, and it only happens when a camera has already detected somebody. Nothing runs continuously. Recognition looks at a short burst of five frames half a second apart rather than one — the first frame after a detection is usually the worst one — and reports the best.

    Your cameras' detection stream is 352x288, where a face is 15–25 pixels — too small to recognise anyone. That is why these plugins pull a full-resolution frame on demand. A camera whose main stream is unreachable cannot serve them, and the plugin list says which.

    Turning it off¶

    Three different "off", and they mean different things:

    Effect
    Disarm stops running; stays installed and configured
    Uninstall removes the plugin and its model; enrolled people remain
    MINDER_PLUGINS=off stops all plugins, whatever is armed

    To remove the biometric data itself, delete the people — uninstalling the plugin does not, on purpose, so that reinstalling does not silently lose them.

    Privacy summary¶

    • Runs entirely on your appliance. No face ever leaves the house.
    • Templates exist only for people someone deliberately enrolled.
    • Photographs are discarded; templates cannot be turned back into faces.
    • Deletion is verified, not asserted.
    • A match can add a name to an alert. It can never remove one.
